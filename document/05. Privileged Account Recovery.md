
# 05. Privileged Account Recovery

## 1. How Can We Recover MFA Without Putting a Privileged Account at Risk?

> What happens when a finance approver or administrator genuinely loses access to their MFA device?
>
> The employee still needs to regain access, but the recovery process must not create an opportunity for someone else to take over the account.
>
> For this scenario, I've designed a 5 step recovery process that separates help desk responsibilities from IAM administration and introduces additional checks before authentication methods can be changed.

## 2. My Proposed Five-Step Recovery Process

| Step | What happens | Responsible team | Why this step matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Record the request** | Create a ticket, confirm the account involved, document the lost device and note any suspicious behaviour. | Help Desk | Provides traceability and an audit trail. |
| **2. Verify identity** | Use an approved independent verification or account recovery method. Personal details alone are insufficient. | Help Desk / Identity Verification Team | Reduces the risk of impersonation and social engineering. |
| **3. Review and authorise** | IAM reviews the verification evidence, account privileges and any red flags. Require independent approval for high-risk recovery. | IAM / Security Approver | Enforces separation of duties and prevents unauthorised recovery. |
| **4. Restore MFA securely** | An authorised IAM administrator uses an approved recovery mechanism, such as a short-lived Microsoft Entra Temporary Access Pass (TAP), to allow registration of a new authenticator. | IAM Team | Restricts recovery access and ensures only authorised personnel perform sensitive changes. |
| **5. Validate and close** | Confirm the new MFA method works, invalidate the lost method, review relevant logs, notify the employee through a trusted channel and document completion. | IAM / Security Team | Detects unexpected changes and confirms recovery was completed securely. |

**Exception handling:** If identity verification fails or suspicious activity remains unresolved, recovery stays on hold and the request is escalated for security review.

## 3. Additional Protective Control — Limiting the Damage

### Just-in-Time Privileged Access

> I would introduce just-in-time privileged access for administrative accounts, using Microsoft Entra Privileged Identity Management (PIM) where appropriate.
>
> Instead of keeping administrative privileges permanently active, eligible users activate them only when needed, subject to configured controls such as approval, MFA and time limits.
>
> If an attacker compromises an account, this can reduce the opportunity to misuse standing administrative permissions.
>
> For finance approvers, a related application-level safeguard would be independent approval for high-risk payments, since Entra PIM does not automatically govern banking transaction permissions.
>
> These controls limit different forms of damage, but neither replaces secure MFA recovery.

## 4. What This Design Changes

> The redesigned process creates a clear distinction between:
>
> - **Verification:** Establishing that the request comes from the legitimate account owner.
> - **Authorisation:** Deciding whether recovery is permitted.
> - **Execution:** Allowing authorised IAM personnel to perform the recovery.
> - **Validation:** Confirming the account has been recovered securely.
>
> The aim is to prevent an urgent help desk request from becoming a shortcut around authentication controls.
