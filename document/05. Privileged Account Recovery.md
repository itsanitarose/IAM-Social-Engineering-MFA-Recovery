# 05. Privileged Account Recovery

## 1. How Can We Recover MFA Without Putting a Privileged Account at Risk?

> What happens when a finance approver or administrator genuinely loses access to their MFA device? They need to regain access, but the recovery process must not give someone else an opportunity to take over the account.
>
> I've designed a five-step recovery process around three rules:
>
> 1. The person verifying identity does not approve the reset.
> 2. The person approving recovery does not execute it.
> 3. Treat account compromise as a possibility and investigate suspicious activity before proceeding.
>
> This risk is not hypothetical. In September 2023, reporting linked the MGM Resorts cyberattack to social engineering of its IT help desk ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-09-16/mgm-resorts-hackers-broke-in-after-tricking-it-service-desk)). The incident demonstrated how manipulating a support process can become an entry point into an organisation.
>
> Each step below addresses a weakness that could allow a similar attack to succeed.

## 2. My Proposed Five-Step Recovery Process

### Step 1 — Record and Triage the Request

**Owner:** Help Desk

- Create a ticket before making changes. Every subsequent action should reference the ticket ID.
- Record the claimed identity, affected account, reason for recovery, calling number and timestamp.
- Compare the incoming number with the HR record. A mismatch is not proof of fraud, but should be documented.
- Identify whether the account has sensitive financial access or administrative privileges.
- Request an authorised IAM analyst to review registered MFA methods, recent sign-ins and available account risk information in Microsoft Entra ID.

**What this prevents:** Rushed, undocumented recovery requests with no reliable audit trail.

### Step 2 — Independently Verify Identity

**Owner:** Help Desk / Identity Verification Team

- Use a previously verified contact method obtained from an authoritative company record, rather than relying on the incoming number.
- If the employee cannot access that channel, use another approved recovery or identity-proofing process.
- Treat employee ID, date of birth and manager's name as identifying information, not proof of account ownership.
- Review suspicious behaviour, including refusal of verification, unusual pressure and inconsistent contact information.
- As a proposed additional safeguard, refer recovery requests for security review if the registered contact number was changed within the previous seven days.

**Technical considerations:**

- A callback provides a more trustworthy contact route than accepting an incoming call, but it does not guarantee identity. SIM swapping, call forwarding and compromised contact records remain possible.
- The seven-day review period is my proposed safeguard, not a Microsoft or NIST requirement.

**What this prevents:** Impersonation using stolen personal information and unsafe reliance on caller-supplied contact details.

### Step 3 — Review and Authorise

**Owner:** IAM Analyst / Independent Security Approver

- Review the verification evidence, account privileges, sign-in risk history and recent authentication method changes.
- Investigate unresolved red flags before authorising recovery.
- Require independent security approval for finance approvers and administrative accounts.
- Record the approval decision, approver identity and supporting evidence in the ticket.
- Ensure the approver and the IAM administrator performing recovery are different individuals.

This separation of duties is part of my proposed Northwind Health recovery policy.

**What this prevents:** One individual having unchecked control over verification, approval and execution, while reducing the risk of suspicious activity being overlooked during recovery.

### Step 4 — Restore MFA Securely

**Owner:** Authorised IAM Administrator

- Use an approved recovery method, such as a Microsoft Entra Temporary Access Pass (TAP), where supported by organisational policy.
- Configure the TAP with the shortest practical lifetime, preferring single-use TAPs where compatible with the required registration process.
- Deliver the TAP securely through an independently verified channel.
- Allow the legitimate employee to register a replacement authenticator.
- Remove or disable the lost authentication method as appropriate.
- Where compromise is suspected, coordinate session revocation and additional containment with Security.
- Assess whether a lost managed device requires action through Microsoft Intune.

**Technical considerations:**

- Microsoft documents a default TAP lifetime of one hour and a default maximum lifetime of eight hours in TAP policy settings.
- Authentication Administrators can manage TAPs for eligible non-admin users. Privileged Authentication Administrators can manage TAPs for eligible administrator accounts, subject to Microsoft's role restrictions.
- Revoking sign-in sessions does not guarantee that every existing access token or application session terminates immediately. Additional application-level containment may be necessary.
- TAP use must comply with configured Conditional Access requirements.

**What this prevents:** Unauthorised authenticator registration, unnecessary exposure of recovery credentials and some risks associated with existing compromised sessions.

### Step 5 — Validate and Close

**Owner:** IAM / Security Team

- Confirm the replacement authenticator was registered successfully.
- Verify that the employee can complete an approved sign-in test.
- Confirm the reported lost method has been removed or disabled where appropriate.
- Review Entra audit logs for unexpected authentication method changes.
- Examine relevant sign-in activity during the recovery period.
- Verify that required containment actions were completed.
- Notify the employee through a trusted channel.
- Close the ticket with verification evidence, approvals and recovery actions documented.

If unexplained activity remains, refer the ticket for security investigation rather than closing it as a successful recovery.

**What this prevents:** Suspicious authentication changes being overlooked simply because the employee can sign in again.

### If Verification Fails

> Pause the MFA recovery and escalate the request for security review.
>
> Failed verification does not automatically mean the account should be locked. Any decision to block sign-ins or revoke access should depend on the assessed risk and evidence of possible compromise.
>
> Delayed recovery may affect availability, but bypassing verification could expose the account to unauthorised access.

## 3. Additional Control — Independent Financial Transaction Approval

Because Dana can approve payments and access the banking portal, my selected additional control is **independent approval for high-risk financial transactions**.

- Require a second authorised individual to approve transactions that meet defined risk criteria.
- Ensure the second approver authenticates independently.
- Prevent the same individual from completing all required approval stages.
- Record payment approvals for auditing and investigation.

**What this limits:** If an attacker compromises Dana's account, that account alone may not be enough to complete a high-risk payment.

This safeguard would not prevent every form of account misuse, particularly access to confidential information or compromise of the second approver.

For administrative accounts, just-in-time access through Microsoft Entra Privileged Identity Management (PIM) is another relevant safeguard. However, PIM does not automatically govern payment permissions inside banking applications.

## 4. Why This Design Holds

| Step | Core Principle | Risk Reduced |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Record & triage | Traceability | Rushed, undocumented requests |
| 2 — Verify identity | Independent verification | Impersonation using stolen information |
| 3 — Review & authorise | Separation of duties | Unauthorised approval and single-person control |
| 4 — Restore securely | Controlled, time-limited recovery | Unauthorised authentication changes and recovery credential exposure |
| 5 — Validate & close | Evidence-based validation | Suspicious activity being missed during recovery |

> **Bottom line:** Verification, approval and execution are separate responsibilities. Successful sign-in alone does not complete the recovery; the team must also review the relevant evidence and confirm that the required controls were followed.
