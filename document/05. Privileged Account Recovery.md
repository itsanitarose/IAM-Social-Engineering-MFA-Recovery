
# 05. Privileged Account Recovery

## 1. How Can We Recover MFA Without Putting a Privileged Account at Risk?

How can we safely restore MFA access for employees who can approve financial transactions or manage critical systems?

I've designed a 5 step recovery process that protects account security while considering the impact of delayed access on business operations.

## 2. My Proposed 5 Step Recovery Process

| Step | Responsible Team | Recovery Actions | Core Security Principle | Risk Reduced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Record and Assess** | Help Desk / IAM | Record the request, caller details and reason for recovery. Compare the calling number with HR records and document any concerns. IAM reviews account permissions, recent sign in activity and registered authentication methods to identify available recovery options. | **Traceability** | Rushed or undocumented recovery requests. |
| **2. Verify Identity** | Help Desk | Follow the approved identity verification process. Determine whether the employee can authenticate using another registered and approved method. If not, use an approved independent identity proofing or recovery process. Do not rely on employee ID, date of birth or manager's name alone. If verification fails or is refused, pause recovery and escalate to IAM or Security. | **Independent Verification** | Someone impersonating an employee using stolen information. |
| **3. Review and Authorise** | IAM / Security | Review the verification evidence, account permissions, recent authentication changes and security concerns. Require independent approval for sensitive financial or administrative accounts. Record the decision before proceeding. | **Separation of Duties** | Account recovery proceeding without proper review or approval. |
| **4. Restore MFA Securely** | Authorised IAM Administrator | Select an approved recovery method. If the verified employee can use an existing registered authenticator, they may be able to register a replacement without a full reset. Otherwise, IAM can issue a controlled Microsoft Entra Temporary Access Pass (TAP) through a verified channel. Remove the lost method as appropriate and assess whether session revocation or device containment is required. | **Controlled Recovery** | Unnecessary MFA resets, authentication changes made without permission and exposure of recovery credentials. |
| **5. Validate and Close** | IAM / Security | Confirm the replacement authentication method works and the lost method is no longer usable where required. Review sign in and audit logs for unexpected changes. Confirm necessary security actions, notify the employee through a trusted channel and document the final outcome. | **Evidence Based Validation** | Suspicious account activity being overlooked during recovery. |



### Important Technical Considerations

- **Existing authentication methods:** Losing a phone does not always mean losing every way to sign in. The employee may still have another approved method set up.

- **Failed verification:** If verification does not pass, recovery stays on hold and goes to IAM or Security. Blocking the account is a separate decision based on the evidence and risk.

- **Temporary Access Pass:** Microsoft sets the default lifetime at 1 hour. TAP must be enabled for the account, issued by an authorised administrator, and used in line with Conditional Access policies.

- **Lost or compromised device:** IAM or Security decides whether to disable the old authentication method and whether any managed device needs further action.

- **Session revocation:** Revoking sessions may not end every existing access token or app session straight away.

- **Proposed Northwind Health policy:** Recovering a sensitive account requires proper identity verification and independent approval before an authorised administrator makes the change..

### 3. Business Continuity Consideration

Security should protect the business, not unnecessarily stop it.

While Dana's MFA recovery is on hold, Finance could use an existing authorised alternate approver to keep payments moving. They would use their own account and permissions, without bypassing security checks.

If no alternate is available, Finance should follow its approved escalation process.

| Situation | Required Response |
|---|---|
| **Caller refuses verification** | Keep recovery on hold, record the refusal and refer the request to IAM or Security. |
| **Registered contact method is unavailable** | Use another approved identity proofing or recovery method. Do not accept an unverified replacement number. |
| **No other authentication method is available** | Follow the approved account recovery process rather than bypassing verification. |
| **Suspicious account activity is detected** | Pause recovery and refer the evidence for security review. Apply containment if justified. |
| **Required approval is unavailable** | Keep the request pending until appropriate authorisation can be obtained. |
| **TAP cannot be issued or used** | Check administrator permissions, TAP configuration and Conditional Access requirements. Do not bypass security policies to complete recovery. |
| **Recovery is interrupted or TAP expires** | Reassess the request and restart the approved recovery process where necessary. |
| **Unexpected authentication changes appear** | Do not close the ticket as successfully resolved. Refer the activity for further investigation. |

## 6. How I Would Test the Process

These tests would help establish whether the proposed process works as intended in an authorised Microsoft Entra environment.

| Test Scenario | Expected Result |
|---|---|
| **Employee has another approved authentication method** | Access can be restored using the existing method where permitted, without an unnecessary MFA reset. |
| **Caller knows personal details but refuses verification** | Recovery is paused and the request is escalated. |
| **Identity verified but approval is missing** | No sensitive authentication changes are performed. |
| **Administrator lacks the required permissions** | Recovery action is denied until an appropriately authorised administrator handles it. |
| **TAP expires or is blocked by policy** | The unsuccessful recovery is handled through the approved process without bypassing security requirements. |
| **Replacement authenticator is registered** | The legitimate employee can authenticate and the expected changes appear in audit logs. |
| **Unexpected authentication activity appears** | The ticket remains open for security review. |
| **Sensitive payment lacks required second approval** | The financial application prevents the payment from being completed. |
| **Dana remains unavailable but an authorised alternate exists** | Eligible financial approvals can continue through the established delegation process without accessing Dana's account. |

**Testing status:** These are proposed tests and expected results. No live Entra recovery or financial application testing has been performed.

## 7. My Takeaway

**A secure MFA recovery process should protect the account without unnecessarily disrupting business operations.**

The technical decision is whether it is safe to restore access. The business decision is how essential work continues while that access is unavailable.

Both matter, but business urgency should never be used to bypass identity verification.
