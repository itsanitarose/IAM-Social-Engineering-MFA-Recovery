# 05. Privileged Account Recovery

## 1. How Can We Recover MFA Without Putting a Privileged Account at Risk?

Restoring MFA access for an employee who approves financial transactions or manages critical systems requires more than a quick reset.

My proposed 5 step process separates identity verification, authorisation and recovery. The aim is to restore access safely while allowing essential business activities to continue through approved alternatives.

## 2. My Proposed 5 Step Recovery Process

| Step | Responsible Team | Recovery Actions | Core Security Principle | Risk Reduced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Record and Assess** | Help Desk / IAM | Record the request, caller details and reason for recovery. Compare the calling number with HR records as supporting information, not proof of identity. IAM reviews account permissions, recent sign in activity and registered authentication methods. | **Traceability** | Rushed or undocumented recovery requests. |
| **2. Verify Identity** | Help Desk | Follow the approved verification process. Check whether the employee can authenticate using another registered and approved method. If not, use an approved independent identity verification or recovery process. Do not rely on employee ID, date of birth or a manager's name alone. If verification fails or is refused, pause recovery and escalate to IAM or Security. | **Independent Verification** | Someone impersonating an employee using stolen information. |
| **3. Review and Authorise** | IAM / Security | Review the verification evidence, account permissions and security concerns. Require independent approval for sensitive financial or administrative accounts. Record the decision before proceeding. | **Separation of Duties** | Unauthorised recovery of a sensitive account. |
| **4. Restore MFA Securely** | Authorised IAM Administrator | Select an approved recovery method. If the verified employee can authenticate using an existing method, a full reset may not be necessary. Otherwise, consider a controlled Microsoft Entra Temporary Access Pass (TAP), delivered through a verified channel. Address the lost authentication method and assess whether session revocation or device containment is needed. | **Controlled Recovery** | Unnecessary resets and exposure of recovery credentials. |
| **5. Validate and Close** | IAM / Security | Confirm the replacement method works and the lost method is no longer usable where required. Review sign in and audit logs for unexpected changes. Notify the employee through a trusted channel and document the outcome. | **Evidence Based Validation** | Suspicious account changes being overlooked after recovery. |

## 3. Exceptions and Business Continuity

**Business urgency should never override identity verification or required approval.**

While Dana's recovery is on hold, Finance could use an existing authorised alternate approver, if one is available. That person would use their own account and permissions through the approved process, not Dana's account.

If no alternate is available, Finance would need to consider its approved escalation options rather than grant access without authorisation.

| Situation | Required Response |
|---|---|
| **Verification fails or the caller refuses it** | Keep recovery on hold, document the concern and escalate to IAM or Security. Blocking the account is a separate decision based on risk. |
| **Registered contact method is unavailable** | Use another approved verification or recovery method. Do not accept a new number supplied by an unverified caller. |
| **No other authentication method is available** | Follow the approved account recovery process rather than bypassing verification. |
| **Required approval is unavailable** | Keep the request pending until the necessary authorisation is obtained. |
| **TAP cannot be issued, is blocked or expires** | Check administrator permissions, TAP settings and Conditional Access requirements. Reassess the request without bypassing security policies. |
| **Suspicious activity or unexpected authentication changes appear** | Pause recovery or keep the ticket open for security review. Apply containment where justified. |

### Important Technical Considerations

- **Existing authentication methods:** Losing a phone does not always mean losing every way to sign in. The employee may still have another approved method.

- **Temporary Access Pass:** Microsoft documents a default lifetime of 1 hour. TAP must be enabled for the account, issued by an appropriately authorised administrator and used in line with Conditional Access policies.

- **Lost or compromised device:** IAM or Security should assess whether the old authentication method needs to be disabled or a managed device requires further action.

- **Session revocation:** Revoking sessions may not immediately end every existing access token or application session.

- **Proposed Northwind Health policy:** Sensitive account recovery requires appropriate identity verification and independent approval before an authorised administrator makes the change.

## 4. Additional Control: Independent Financial Transaction Approval

Because Dana can approve payments and access the banking portal, I would introduce **independent approval for high risk financial transactions**.

Qualifying payments would require approval from a second authorised employee using their own account. This requirement should be enforced within the banking system or financial application.

**What this limits:** If an attacker gains access to Dana's account, that account alone would not be enough to complete a payment requiring two independent approvals.

This would reduce the risk of fraudulent payments, although it would not prevent other forms of account misuse, such as accessing confidential financial information.

## 5. How I Would Test the Process

I would use the following scenarios to assess whether the proposed process works as intended.

| Test Scenario | Expected Result |
|---|---|
| **The employee has another approved authentication method** | The existing method can be used where permitted, avoiding an unnecessary full reset. |
| **The caller knows personal details but refuses verification** | Recovery is paused, documented and escalated. |
| **Dana passes identity verification, but approval is still pending** | MFA recovery remains on hold until an authorised person approves the request. |
| **The administrator lacks the required permissions** | No authentication changes are made until an appropriately authorised administrator handles the request. |
| **TAP expires or is blocked by policy** | Recovery follows the approved process without weakening security requirements. |
| **A replacement authenticator is registered** | The employee can authenticate, the expected changes appear in audit logs and any unusual activity is reviewed. |
| **Dana remains unavailable during a payment approval** | An authorised alternate could use their own account through the approved process, if one is available. |
| **A qualifying payment has only one approval** | The financial system prevents completion until the required second approval is provided. |

**Testing status:** These are proposed tests and expected results. I have not performed a live Microsoft Entra recovery or financial application test.

## 6. My Takeaway

The technical decision is whether access can be restored safely. The business decision is how essential work continues while that access is unavailable.

A secure recovery process must address both without allowing urgency to bypass verification, authorisation or accountability.
