
# 06. Digging Deeper

## 1. What Happens After the Suspicious Call?

The caller failed identity verification, the MFA reset was not approved, and the Help Desk escalated the request to Security.

Although the immediate attempt was stopped, I would not assume Dana's account was safe. The caller could try another Help Desk agent or may already have access through another route.

## 2. What I Would Do Next

| Step | My Actions | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Review the Escalation** | I would review the Help Desk ticket, the failed verification checks and the warning signs. I would preserve available call recordings, timestamps and relevant evidence, and confirm that no MFA reset, TAP or other authentication change had occurred. | Understand what happened and whether urgent action is needed. |
| **2. Contact Dana** | I would contact Dana through a trusted channel already held by Northwind Health, not the number provided by the caller. I would ask whether she requested the reset or noticed anything unusual. | Establish whether the request was legitimate. |
| **3. Check Account Activity** | I would work with IAM or Security to review Entra sign in and audit logs for unfamiliar access or authentication changes. I would also look for repeated recovery requests. If anything suggested wider access, I would check relevant email, application and payment activity with the responsible teams. | Find out whether the caller tried or succeeded through another route. |
| **4. Act on the Findings** | If there were signs of compromise, I would escalate for immediate protective action, such as restricting access or revoking sessions. If no compromise was found, I would document what had been checked and consider whether further monitoring was necessary. | Respond to the actual risk rather than making assumptions. |
| **5. Record and Share What We Learned** | I would document the evidence, findings and actions taken. I would also warn other Help Desk agents about the tactics used and suggest improvements if the investigation exposed a weakness. | Help prevent another attempt from succeeding. |

**If evidence of active compromise appeared at any point, I would escalate immediately rather than wait until every check was finished.**

## 3. How Would the Findings Affect My Response?

| What I Find | What I Would Do |
|---|---|
| **Suspicious call, no evidence of compromise** | Record the attempt, alert relevant Help Desk staff and assess whether further monitoring is needed. |
| **Evidence is unclear** | Continue or escalate the investigation. I would not assume the account was safe just because nothing obvious appeared in the logs. |
| **Unauthorised access or changes** | Escalate urgently for containment and involve Finance if payment approvals or banking activity could be affected. |

I would not automatically lock Dana's account or reset her credentials just because of the call. Those decisions should depend on the evidence and the assessed risk.

## 4. Beyond the Failed MFA Reset

Stopping the MFA reset was the right first step, but I would still want to know whether the account had been targeted elsewhere.

For me, the important part is following the evidence, protecting Dana's account and considering the impact on Finance without causing unnecessary disruption.
