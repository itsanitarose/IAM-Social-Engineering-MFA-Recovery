# 02. Red Flags & Risk Assessment

## 1. Initial Assessment

The help desk escalated an urgent MFA reset request for Dana Whitfield, VP of Finance, after the agent became concerned about the caller's behaviour.

The caller answered several personal questions correctly but declined further verification and asked the agent not to call back.

**The help desk was right to escalate rather than treat this as a routine MFA reset.**

As the IAM team reviewing the escalation, our task is to assess the evidence, identify the potential threat and determine whether the reset can safely proceed.

I started by reviewing the call notes for warning signs.

## 2. Warning Signs

| Red Flag                              | Why It Matters                                                                                                                             |
| :------------------------------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Unrecognised phone number**         | The UK calling number does not match HR records. This needs checking, although it does not establish the caller's location.                |
| **Callback refused**                  | The caller asked the agent not to call back, resisting verification through an independently recorded contact number.                      |
| **Video call declined**               | The caller said her camera was broken. This adds to the concerns, although a video call alone would not prove identity.                    |
| **Urgency and authority**             | References to the CEO and a board meeting in 20 minutes put pressure on the agent to act quickly.                                          |
| **Knowledge of personal details**     | The correct employee ID, manager's name and date of birth made the claim convincing, but these details are not reliable proof of identity. |
| **Travel information needs checking** | Dana's Austin meetings raise questions about her travel story but do not establish where she is.                                           |
| **New MFA device requested**          | Registering a replacement phone could give an unauthorised person control of an authentication method on Dana's account.                   |
| **Privileged Finance access**         | The account can approve payments and access the bank portal, increasing the potential impact of an unauthorised reset.                     |

Looking at these details together, I have enough evidence to treat the request as a serious security concern.

## 3. Risk Assessment

**Risk level: High**

I assessed this request as high risk because it combines three concerns:

- **Unverified identity:** The caller provided correct personal details but did not complete independent verification.
- **Suspected social engineering:** The caller used urgency and executive authority while resisting further checks.
- **Potential financial impact:** An unauthorised MFA reset could expose payment approvals and banking access.

## 4. My Decision

My experience in billing and payment support taught me that correct personal details do not automatically authorise an account change. If identity cannot be verified, the request cannot proceed.

I would apply the same principle here, but as an IAM analyst, my responsibility goes further. I need to consider not only the identity verification concerns, but also the suspected social engineering attempt and the potential impact on a privileged Finance account.

**Based on my assessment, I would not authorise the MFA reset.**

The request should remain on hold while the caller's identity is independently verified and the security concerns are resolved through the approved recovery process.
