
# 04. Identity Verification Methods

## 1. How Do We Know the Caller Is Really Dana?

The caller provided the correct employee ID, manager's name and date of birth. But does knowing these details actually prove her identity?

I assessed six verification methods using NIST's Digital Identity Guidelines, particularly **SP 800-63A-4** (Identity Proofing) and **SP 800-63B-4** (Authentication and Account Recovery).

## 2. Strong vs Weak Verification Methods

| Verification Method | Rating | NIST Aligned Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Employee ID** | **Weak** | Identifies an employee in company records but does not prove the caller owns the account. |
| **Date of birth** | **Weak** | Personal information can be obtained through public records, data breaches or social engineering. It is not reliable authentication evidence. |
| **Callback to verified number** | **Strong (conditional)** | Uses a previously established contact channel rather than the incoming number. However, telephone based verification can be affected by SIM swapping, call forwarding or compromised contact records. |
| **Manager approval through a known channel** | **Strong (supporting)** | Independently confirms the business need and authorisation. However, managerial approval does not independently establish the caller's identity. |
| **Live video call showing photo ID** | **Strong (conditional)** | Can support identity proofing when a trained agent validates the document and verifies the person presenting it through an approved process. A simple visual check alone is insufficient. |
| **Security questions** | **Weak** | Answers may be guessed, researched or obtained through social engineering. NIST does not recognise traditional knowledge based security questions as acceptable authenticators. |

*These ratings are my assessment informed by NIST guidance, not ratings explicitly assigned by NIST. A method rated Strong is not necessarily sufficient on its own.*

## 3. My Takeaway

The biggest concern in Dana's case is that the caller knew several personal details, but refused additional verification.

NIST distinguishes between identifying someone, validating identity evidence and verifying that the person presenting the evidence is its rightful owner.

For an account with sensitive financial access, I would prioritise an existing trusted authenticator or approved recovery method where available.

If those are unavailable, the request should follow an appropriate identity proofing process with additional authorisation.

**My decision: Successful identity verification allows the recovery request to be reviewed. It does not automatically authorise an MFA reset.**
