
# 04. Identity Verification Methods

## 1. How Do We Know the Caller Is Really Dana?

The caller provided the correct employee ID, manager's name and date of birth. But does knowing these details actually prove her identity?

I assessed five verification methods using NIST's Digital Identity Guidelines to understand which provide stronger assurance and which could be exploited through social engineering.

## 2. Strong vs Weak Verification Methods

| Verification Method | Rating | NIST-Aligned Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Employee ID | **Weak** | Identifies an employee but does not prove the caller's identity. |
| Date of birth | **Weak** | Personal information is not reliable authentication evidence. |
| Callback to verified number | **Strong (conditional)** | Uses a previously established contact channel, although telephone based verification has security limitations. |
| Manager approval | **Strong (supporting)** | Supports authorisation but does not independently prove identity. |
| Video call showing photo ID | **Weak on its own** | Visual inspection alone is insufficient without a controlled identity proofing process. |

*These ratings are my assessment based on NIST guidance, not ratings explicitly assigned by NIST. Strong methods may still need additional safeguards.*

## 3. My Takeaway

Correct personal details are not enough to establish identity. For an account with sensitive financial access, I would require an approved recovery process rather than relying on one informal verification check.
